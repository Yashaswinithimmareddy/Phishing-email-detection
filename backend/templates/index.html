        How to Spot a Phishing Email
        10 Critical Forensic Indicators Every User & Analyst Must Check
        Sender Address & Domain

        Never rely solely on the friendly display name. Look closely at the actual email address after the @ symbol for subtle misspellings (e.g., paypa1.com).

        Artificial Urgency & Fear

        Phrases like "Within 24 hours" or "Immediate suspension" are psychological tricks designed to bypass logical deliberation.

        Direct Credential Requests

        Legitimate financial institutions and IT services will never send emails asking you to confirm your password or OTP.

        Raw IP or Misleading URLs

        Hover without clicking. Watch out for raw numeric IP addresses (http://198.51.100.10) or URL shorteners masking the true destination.

        Dangerous Double Extensions

        Be suspicious of attachments like invoice.pdf.exe or resume.docx.vbs which disguise malicious executables as benign documents.

        Generic Salutations

        Greetings like "Dear Valued Customer" or "Dear User" occur in mass spam where attackers do not know your identity.

        SOC Operational Metrics
        Why Precision & Recall Matter More Than Accuracy
        Precision in Phishing

        Precision measures: Of all emails flagged as phishing, how many were truly malicious? Low precision floods SOC analysts with false alerts and mistakenly quarantines valid customer orders.

        Recall in Phishing

        Recall measures: Of all actual phishing attacks, how many did we catch? Low recall produces false negatives, allowing credential theft or malware inside corporate perimeters.

        Hybrid Detection Advantage

        Combining interpretable rule-based heuristics with machine learning probability provides explainability for human triage while capturing complex linguistic nuances.
